The stairway descended into darkness older than memory. Kael went first, his torch
casting jumping shadows across walls carved with faces that had no eyes. Lyra
followed, one hand trailing the stone, her lips moving silently over a ward she
had cast and recast seventeen times since the surface.

[SFX: heavy_stone_door_grinding]

The door at the bottom had not been opened in a thousand years. It ground open
under Kael's hand, a deep rumble that shook dust from the ceiling and sent it
drifting down in slow grey curtains.

[Kael] "Stay behind me. Whatever's in here, it's been waiting."

[Lyra] "That's what worries me. Things that wait are patient. Patient things plan."

The chamber beyond was vast — a cathedral of stone, its ceiling lost in darkness,
its floor a mosaic of cracked tile showing a constellation that no longer existed.
At the center stood a sarcophagus, and around it, arranged in a perfect circle,
seven suits of armor, standing as if their wearers had simply stopped mid-step and
turned to stone.

[SFX: rocks_falling_cave_in]

Kael's boot touched the first tile past the threshold. The sound was small — a
click, no louder than a key turning. Then the ceiling above the stairway gave way.

Rocks the size of skulls thundered down, sealing the exit in a roar of splintering
stone and choking dust. Lyra threw herself flat. Kael caught a falling chunk on his
shield, and the impact drove him to one knee, but he held.

[Kael] "Well. No going back."

[Lyra] "They don't want us to leave. That means we're close."

The seven suits of armor turned their heads. Not all at once — one, then two, then
the rest, in a slow ripple, like something waking up joint by joint. The first
raised a sword that had no rust on it, though it had stood in the dark for
centuries.

[SFX: sword_clash_combat]

Kael met it before it finished swinging. Steel rang against steel in a sound that
filled the cathedral and came back from every wall. The thing was strong — stronger
than a man should be without breath or heartbeat — but Kael had fought things
without breath before. He let it overextend, stepped inside its guard, and took its
head from its shoulders with a single backhand cut. The body stood for a moment,
then collapsed in a clatter of empty metal.

[Kael] "One. Six to go."

[Lyra] "No. Watch."

The fallen armor was already putting itself back together. The helmet rolled toward
the body. The gauntlets twitched.

[Lyra] "They won't stop. They're not alive — they're a command. We have to break
the command."

She stepped forward, into the circle, and raised both hands. The air around her
fingers turned white, then blue, then a color that had no name — the color of
something seen from the corner of the eye, there and not there.

[SFX: magical_explosion_energy_burst]

What she released was not a spell in any book Kael had ever seen. It was a word,
spoken in a voice that was not entirely hers, and it struck the sarcophagus like a
hammer striking a bell. The sound that came back was not sound — it was pressure,
and silence, and the feeling of something very old letting go.

The six remaining armors froze. Then, one by one, they fell — not collapsing, but
dissolving, rust racing across their surfaces in seconds, metal flaking to orange
dust, until there was nothing left but seven piles of powder on the mosaic floor.

The sarcophagus lid cracked down the center. Inside, there was no body. There was a
light — small, steady, and very, very old.

[Kael] "What is it?"

[Lyra] "I think it's what they were guarding. I think it's what they were afraid
of someone finding."

[Kael] "Is it safe?"

[Lyra] "No. Not remotely. But neither was the door, and we opened it anyway."

She reached in. The light climbed her arm, and for just a moment, both of them
disappeared — and the cathedral was empty, and the dust settled, and the silence
that had kept its vigil for a thousand years settled back over the stones like a
blanket pulled over a sleeping face.
